Karnataka permit timelines: a realist's view

SPC Liaison Team 7 min read

Every client asks the same question at the start: how soon can we break ground? The honest answer in Karnataka is, "it depends on which approvals you already have, and which you don't." We've moved 29 projects through statutory clearances across KIADB, BBMP, Bommasandra, Hoskote and Devanahalli — and the gap between optimistic timelines and actual ones is where most projects lose their first quarter.

Here's what each clearance realistically takes, in our experience, and how we sequence them to minimise idle time.

KIADB allotment & possession

If your land is on a KIADB allotted plot, you're already past one of the longest queues. Possession letter and lease deed take 4-8 weeks after allotment payment. We've seen this stretch on disputed plots or where neighbouring parties have unresolved issues — KIADB will wait those out before issuing possession.

Tip: get the lease deed in hand before submitting building plan — many downstream approvals reference the lease registration date.

Building plan sanction

For KIADB plots, plan sanction goes through KIADB itself (faster, more predictable than BBMP). Expect 6-10 weeks from submission to sanction for a clean plan. For BBMP plots inside Bengaluru, the timeline is more variable: 8-16 weeks is typical.

What slows it down: FAR (Floor Area Ratio) calculations that need clarification, setback queries, or fire NoC pre-requisites not yet attached.

Environmental clearances

  • KSPCB Consent for Establishment (CFE) — required before construction starts on any project above the relevant size threshold. 6-10 weeks typical
  • KSPCB Consent for Operation (CFO) — needed before commissioning, not before construction. Plan to file this in month 7-8 of an 12-month project
  • EIA Clearance — only triggered for large or specific-category projects. If you need this, factor in 4-6 months

Fire NoC (KSFES)

Fire Department approval is a two-stage process. Provisional NoC at plan stage takes 3-6 weeks. Final NoC after construction (post fire-fighting installation) takes another 2-4 weeks. The fire fighting design must align with the approved plan — last-minute changes mean re-submission.

Electrical (BESCOM)

Power sanction is the timeline killer most clients underestimate. For sub-1MVA loads, BESCOM takes 6-10 weeks. For HT loads (1MVA+) or dedicated transformers, expect 3-5 months end-to-end including bay allocation, transformer ordering, and energisation. Start this early — it's almost always the last thing ready and the first thing that delays handover.

Other approvals you'll hit

  • Lift inspector — for buildings with elevators, factor 2-3 weeks
  • BWSSB water connection — outside KIADB, 4-6 weeks
  • Pollution control air/water consent — if your operations need it, allocate 4-8 weeks
  • Occupancy Certificate — final clearance to use the building, 4-8 weeks after construction completion
"On a recent Devanahalli aerospace facility, we filed plan sanction and fire NoC the same week. They came back in parallel. Six weeks saved versus filing them sequentially. That's the difference between a 12-month and a 13-month project."

How to sequence to avoid lost time

  • Parallel-file everything you can — plan sanction, fire NoC, KSPCB CFE — these don't depend on each other
  • Front-load BESCOM — file power sanction in month 1, not month 6
  • Use the liaison expertise of your GC — we've filed hundreds of these. The forms, the right submissions, the right people to follow up with — that's institutional knowledge
  • Don't optimise approvals timeline at the cost of design quality — re-submission after rejection costs more time than getting it right first time

The honest summary

Plan for 10-14 weeks of statutory clearance overhead on any Karnataka industrial project, even with good liaison and clean plans. For projects without prior KIADB experience or with EIA triggers, budget 18-26 weeks. The clients who get burned are the ones who assume "we have the land, let's break ground in 4 weeks." We've never seen that happen in Karnataka. We've also never seen a well-sequenced statutory plan take longer than 14 weeks.

That's the gap. The difference between knowing it and not knowing it is your first quarter.
