PEB explained: when, why and how much

Dr. Sudharshan B S — Technical Consultant 5 min read

Pre-Engineered Buildings are everywhere now. Walk through KIADB Devanahalli, Hoskote logistics parks, or any industrial corridor around Bengaluru and you'll see them — the long-span steel-framed warehouses, factories, and aviation MROs that went from foundation to ready-to-occupy in 5-6 months. PEB is the reason that schedule is possible.

But PEB isn't the right answer for every project. We've delivered PEB on flagship work like SATS Food Solutions and Wildcraft India. We've also turned down PEB recommendations for projects where it would have cost more for less. Here's the framework we use.

What PEB actually is

A Pre-Engineered Building is a steel structure designed and fabricated off-site, then assembled at the project location. The columns, rafters, and roof purlins are engineered as a complete system — typically tapered to optimise steel weight against span and load. It arrives at site as numbered components and erects in weeks, not months.

The opposite is conventional RCC (reinforced concrete) — where columns, beams, and slabs are cast on-site, requiring shuttering, curing time, and sequential floor-by-floor construction.

Where PEB wins

  • Long clear spans — warehouses, factories, hangars where you need 25-40m spans without columns
  • Single-storey or low-rise — typically up to 2 levels efficiently
  • Schedule-critical — when every month of delay costs the client revenue
  • Standardised geometry — rectangular footprints, regular bay spacing
  • Future flexibility — PEB is easier to expand, reconfigure, or relocate

Where PEB loses

  • Multi-storey offices — RCC is more economical above 3 floors
  • Complex geometry — curves, atria, irregular footprints — RCC adapts better
  • Heavy floor loads — manufacturing with vibrating machinery often needs RCC mass
  • Food-grade or controlled environment — PEB shell still needs heavy insulation, controlled openings
  • Architectural focus — exposed-concrete aesthetic or signature design
"On Wildcraft India's 289,000 sft Tumakuru facility, PEB cut six weeks off the original RCC schedule. On Ananth Technologies, the multi-level office portion needed RCC — but the warehouse block ran PEB. Same project, two systems. That's the right answer most of the time."

The honest cost picture

People ask: is PEB cheaper? Yes and no. The structural steel cost per sft is often higher than equivalent RCC. But:

  • You save 30-40% on schedule
  • You save on shuttering, scaffolding, and concrete pour cycles
  • You save on financing cost (one less quarter of EMI)
  • You save on lost-revenue cost (one less quarter of empty facility)

For SPC's clients, the all-in cost picture usually lands PEB 5-12% cheaper for the right project — once you count what the client pays for time.

How we decide on every project

For every brief that crosses our desk, our technical team runs three checks:

  • Span and storeys — under 3 levels and over 20m span → PEB likely wins
  • Schedule pressure — if the client has revenue locked behind go-live date, PEB wins
  • Loading and use — heavy machinery vibration, food-grade, or specialised acoustic needs may push back to RCC

The right answer is rarely "all PEB" or "all RCC." It's usually a hybrid — and that's where having one GC across the whole scope (versus two separate vendors for steel and concrete) pays for itself.
